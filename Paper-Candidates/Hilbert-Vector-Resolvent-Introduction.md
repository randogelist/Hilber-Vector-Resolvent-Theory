# An introduction to Hilbert-Vector-Resolvent theory

*Paper candidate · Draft 0.1 · 28 October 2026*

## 1. Introduction

Hilbert-Vector-Resolvent theory is the name used in this project for a framework connecting moment sequences, vector representations, and analytic resolvents. Its starting point is a prescribed sequence of scalar quantities. The first question is whether those quantities admit a representation by a vector acted on by a contraction in a Hilbert space. The second is whether the resulting scalar resolvent agrees with an independently specified analytic function. These are separate mathematical obligations: an abstract operator model must be constructed, and its relation to the intended function must be proved.

The basic object is a triple $(\mathcal H,T,v)$, consisting of a complex Hilbert space, a bounded operator, and a distinguished vector. Iterating the operator produces the matrix coefficients

$$
c_n=\langle v,T^n v\rangle.
$$

When $T$ is a contraction, the coefficients are uniformly bounded by $\|v\|^2$. Their generating function is therefore holomorphic in the open unit disk and has the resolvent representation

$$
F(z)=\langle v,(I-zT)^{-1}v\rangle.
$$

The distinguished vector is essential. The scalar function records the action of $T$ visible from that vector; it need not record the action of $T$ on the whole space. For self-adjoint $T$, the relevant part is the cyclic subspace generated by $v$. No identification of the entire operator or its entire spectrum is asserted without an appropriate cyclicity condition.

A stronger structure appears when $T$ is a positive self-adjoint contraction. By the spectral theorem, the coefficients are real moments of a positive spectral measure supported in $[0,1]$. Conversely, a probability measure on $[0,1]$ gives a concrete model: the Hilbert space is $L^2$ of the measure, the operator is multiplication by the coordinate, and the distinguished vector is the constant function one. The resulting scalar resolvent is an integral transform. Thus moment positivity, vector geometry, and analytic sign conditions become different descriptions of the same represented data.

The arithmetic application considered here begins with the actual normalized logarithmic-derivative moments of the Riemann $\xi$-function. Finite Bernstein identities associate a row of weights to each order. Their cancellation controls total variation, and the semantic zero-cancellation condition $H_{00}$ forces every Bernstein weight to be nonnegative. The existing formal development constructs a probability measure with exactly those moments. Its resolvent then agrees with the actual logarithmic derivative near the origin. Continuing an analytic defect and examining the local poles excludes nonreal zeros of the centered-square entire function, giving the Riemann hypothesis.

The contribution of this introduction is to organize these steps around a precise mathematical framework. The operator and moment-representation principles are classical. The project-specific content lies in the actual sequence, its calibration, the finite cancellation identities, and the verified implication from the exact premise $H_{00}$. The existence of a generic contractive model by itself gives no conclusion about the Riemann zeta function.

## 2. Vector orbits and scalar resolvents

We fix the convention that the complex inner product is conjugate-linear in the first argument and linear in the second. Let $T$ be a bounded linear operator on $\mathcal H$ with $\|T\|\le1$, and let $v\in\mathcal H$. Put

$$
c_n=\langle v,T^n v\rangle,\qquad c_0=\|v\|^2.
$$

Cauchy–Schwarz and contractivity give

$$
|c_n|\le \|v\|\,\|T^n v\|\le \|v\|^2.
$$

The geometric majorant proves absolute convergence of $\sum_{n\ge0}c_nz^n$ for $|z|<1$. The operator Neumann series converges in norm there, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(z)
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_nz^n
 =\left\langle v,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^nT^n v\right\rangle\\
&=\langle v,(I-zT)^{-1}v\rangle,
\qquad
|F(z)|\le\frac{\|v\|^2}{1-|z|}.
\end{aligned}
$$

These estimates require contractivity and the Hilbert-space inequalities; positivity and self-adjointness are not needed for this disk argument.

The same coefficient estimate has a useful decomposition. Writing $x=T^nv$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|v\|^4-|\langle v,x\rangle|^2
={}&\|v\|^2\bigl(\|v\|^2-\|x\|^2\bigr)\\
&+\bigl(\|v\|^2\|x\|^2-|\langle v,x\rangle|^2\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

Both terms on the right are nonnegative. The first is the contraction defect; the second is the Gram determinant of the pair $(v,x)$. This explains why coefficient bounds can be derived from an abstract geometry retaining only norm-square and Cauchy–Schwarz information. The existing Lean Gram-orbit interface uses precisely such weaker data. It should not be identified with a complete Hilbert-space structure.

## 3. Positivity and moment representations

Suppose now that $T=T^*$, $0\le T\le I$, and $\|v\|=1$. We call such a triple a **positive Hilbert-vector model**. Its moments

$$
u_n=\langle v,T^nv\rangle
$$

satisfy $u_0=1$ and $0\le u_n\le1$. More generally, the functional calculus for the positive contraction gives

$$
\sum_{r=0}^{k}(-1)^r\binom{k}{r}u_{n+r}
=\langle v,T^n(I-T)^kv\rangle\ge0.
$$

Indeed, the polynomial $q^n(1-q)^k$ is nonnegative on $[0,1]$, which contains the spectrum of $T$. The Bernstein weights

$$
w_{N,j}
=\binom Nj\sum_{r=0}^{N-j}(-1)^r
                 \binom{N-j}{r}u_{j+r},
\qquad 0\le j\le N,
$$

are consequently nonnegative. Their sum is $u_0=1$.

In the direction needed for the actual arithmetic application, the starting point is instead a real sequence whose Bernstein row weights are already known to be nonnegative. The accompanying Hausdorff construction proves that normalized rows with nonnegative weights yield a Borel probability measure $\sigma$ on $[0,1]$ with

$$
u_n=\int_{[0,1]}q^n\,d\sigma(q).
$$

Briefly, the positive Bernstein averages define uniformly bounded positive functionals on continuous functions. A single fixed free ultrafilter gives a limiting positive functional. Riesz representation supplies the measure, and the proved monomial-limit identities identify all its moments. No representing measure is assumed in this construction.

From this measure the concrete Hilbert-vector model is immediate:

$$
\mathcal H=L^2([0,1],\sigma;\mathbb C),\qquad
(Tf)(q)=qf(q),\qquad v(q)=1.
$$

Multiplication by the bounded real function $q$ is self-adjoint. Moreover,

$$
0\le\langle f,Tf\rangle
 =\int q|f(q)|^2\,d\sigma(q)
 \le\|f\|^2.
$$

Thus $0\le T\le I$, $\|v\|^2=\sigma([0,1])=1$, and the prescribed sequence is represented exactly:

$$
\langle v,T^nv\rangle=\int q^n\,d\sigma(q)=u_n.
$$

This concrete $L^2$ construction is the operator interpretation used in the present candidate. The existing project formalization constructs the measure and its scalar resolvent; it does not yet package this $L^2$ realization as a separate audited Lean construction.

## 4. The sign of the scaled resolvent

For a positive Hilbert-vector model and $\kappa>0$, define

$$
S_\kappa(z)
=\kappa\langle v,(I+\kappa zT)^{-1}v\rangle.
$$

Since $\operatorname{spec}(T)\subseteq[0,1]$, this is holomorphic on the common domain

$$
D_\kappa=\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,-1/\kappa].
$$

A particular operator may permit a larger resolvent domain. The displayed slit plane is a domain guaranteed for every positive contraction, including $z=0$.

The nonpositive imaginary-part condition follows directly from the operator geometry. For $z\in D_\kappa$, set $y=(I+\kappa zT)^{-1}v$. Then

$$
v=y+\kappa zTy,
\qquad
\langle v,y\rangle
=\|y\|^2+\kappa\overline z\,\langle Ty,y\rangle.
$$

Self-adjointness makes $\langle Ty,y\rangle=\langle y,Ty\rangle$ real, and positivity makes it nonnegative. Therefore

$$
\operatorname{Im}S_\kappa(z)
=-\kappa^2\operatorname{Im}z\,\langle y,Ty\rangle
\le0\qquad(\operatorname{Im}z>0).
$$

In the multiplication model, this is exactly the scalar integral identity

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_\kappa(z)
&=\kappa\int_{[0,1]}\frac{d\sigma(q)}{1+\kappa qz},\\
\operatorname{Im}S_\kappa(z)
&=-\kappa^2\operatorname{Im}z
  \int_{[0,1]}\frac{q}{|1+\kappa qz|^2}\,d\sigma(q).
\end{aligned}
$$

The sign is nonpositive, not necessarily strictly negative. The normalization matters: the unscaled generating resolvent $F(z)=\langle v,(I-zT)^{-1}v\rangle$ has nonnegative imaginary part in the upper half-plane.

## 5. Calibration to the actual $\xi$-function

Let $\xi$ be the actual entire Riemann $\xi$-function used in the formal development, and let $\Theta$ be its entire centered-square descent:

$$
\Theta(w^2)=\xi\!\left(\tfrac12+w\right).
$$

The retained actual-function results give $\Theta(0)\ne0$. Thus $R=\Theta'/\Theta$ is analytic near zero. Define

$$
m_n=\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\frac{(-1)^n}{n!}R^{(n)}(0)\right),
\qquad
\kappa=m_0>0,\qquad
u_n=\frac{m_n}{\kappa^{n+1}}.
$$

The reality and positivity facts used here are proved for the actual functions in the retained development. In particular $u_0=1$, and

$$
R^{(n)}(0)=(-1)^n n!\,\kappa^{n+1}u_n.
$$

For the Bernstein rows of this actual sequence, let $V_N=\sum_{j=0}^N|w_{N,j}|$. The row cancellation $C_n$ is the sum of nonnegative triangle defects arising from Bernstein coarsening. It satisfies the finite identity

$$
C_n=(n+1)(V_{n+1}-V_n).
$$

The condition $H_{00}$ is the assertion that $C_n=0$ for every $n$. Equivalently, it is semantic admissibility of the cutoff-zero, coefficient-zero cancellation budget. The identities $V_0=1$ and $\sum_jw_{N,j}=1$ then give

$$
H_{00}
\ \Longleftrightarrow\ 
\forall N,\ V_N=1
\ \Longleftrightarrow\ 
\forall N,\ \forall j\le N,\ w_{N,j}\ge0.
$$

Under $H_{00}$, the constructed measure has these exact actual moments. Its resolvent satisfies

$$
S_\kappa^{(n)}(0)
=(-1)^n n!\,\kappa^{n+1}\int q^n\,d\sigma(q)
=R^{(n)}(0).
$$

Analyticity turns equality of derivatives into equality of germs. The identity is continued through the analytic function $\Theta'-S_\kappa\Theta$ on the connected upper half-plane. This step is valid across possible zeros of $\Theta$; it does not assume that the logarithmic derivative is already holomorphic there. Away from zeros, it gives $\Theta'/\Theta=S_\kappa$.

At an upper-half-plane zero $\rho$ of multiplicity $m\ge1$, local factorization would give

$$
\frac{\Theta'(z)}{\Theta(z)}
=\frac{m}{z-\rho}+B(z),
$$

where $B$ is holomorphic near $\rho$. On the downward probe $z=\rho-i\varepsilon$, the principal part has imaginary component $m/\varepsilon>0$, contradicting the nonpositive sign of $S_\kappa$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. Conjugate-zero symmetry excludes lower-half-plane zeros as well. The centered-square relation, together with the retained nonzero imaginary part of every $\xi$-zero, then forces each such zero to have real part $1/2$.

This is the completed measure–resolvent implication $H_{00}\Rightarrow\mathrm{RH}$. A second completed forward construction passes through constant row variation and the actual spectral criterion. Both have the same retained converse $\mathrm{RH}\Rightarrow H_{00}$. The equivalences do not prove $H_{00}$ unconditionally.

## 6. Weighted vectors and the next identification problem

The measure also supports a useful family of contractive models. For $a>0$, put

$$
w_a(q)=\frac{4\kappa a}{(1+\kappa aq)^2},
\qquad
\lambda_a(q)=\frac{4\kappa aq}{(1+\kappa aq)^2}.
$$

On $[0,1]$, we have $0<w_a(q)\le4\kappa a$ and $0\le\lambda_a(q)\le1$; the latter bound follows from $(1-\kappa aq)^2\ge0$. Hence $v_a=\sqrt{w_a}$ belongs to $L^2(\sigma)$, and multiplication by $\lambda_a$ defines a positive contraction $T_a$. Its coefficients and generating resolvent are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde\mu_n(a)
&=\langle v_a,T_a^nv_a\rangle
 =\int w_a(q)\lambda_a(q)^n\,d\sigma(q),\\
G_a(h)
&=\langle v_a,(I-hT_a)^{-1}v_a\rangle
 =\int\frac{w_a(q)}{1-h\lambda_a(q)}\,d\sigma(q),
\qquad |h|<1.
\end{aligned}
$$

These definitions give $0\le\widetilde\mu_n(a)\le\widetilde\mu_0(a)$ and disk holomorphy. They provide a precise model to which an intended coefficient family may be compared.

The tilde is deliberate. Identification with the archived actual Widder coefficients remains a separate obligation in the longer angular construction. The bounds of this model alone do not discharge that identification. The completed measure–resolvent proof in Section 5 uses the actual origin moments and does not require completion of that longer chain.

## 7. Scope of the proposed theory

The framework separates three tasks: obtaining positive or contractive data from a prescribed scalar sequence; constructing a vector or measure representation; and identifying the resulting resolvent with the intended analytic object. Each step needs its own hypotheses and proof. This separation makes the actual mathematical content of an existence assertion visible.

For the present RH application, the conditional scalar measure–resolvent chain and both $H_{00}$-equivalence declarations have passed the preserved Lean build and axiom audit. The Hilbert-space presentation in this candidate is justified mathematically above but is not a newly audited Lean implementation. Its formal construction is a natural next task if the operator presentation is to become part of the verified interface.

The unconditional admissibility of $H_{00}$, a finite accepted witness, and a terminating finite checker's soundness and completeness remain open. Those obligations cannot be replaced by a finite list of successful rows or by an abstract model whose actual-function identification has not been established.
